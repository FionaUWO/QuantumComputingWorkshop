The whole computation takes place in a plane. Define the normalised superpositions of the non-solutions and of the solutions,

$$
|\alpha\rangle = \frac{1}{\sqrt{N - M}} \sum_{f(x) = 0} |x\rangle, \qquad
|\beta\rangle = \frac{1}{\sqrt{M}} \sum_{f(x) = 1} |x\rangle .
$$

They are orthonormal, and the starting state lies in their span:

$$
|s\rangle = \sqrt{\tfrac{N-M}{N}}\,|\alpha\rangle + \sqrt{\tfrac{M}{N}}\,|\beta\rangle = \cos\tfrac\theta2\,|\alpha\rangle + \sin\tfrac\theta2\,|\beta\rangle,
\qquad \sin\tfrac\theta2 = \sqrt{\tfrac{M}{N}} .
$$

In this plane the oracle is the reflection across the line through $|\alpha\rangle$, because it negates $|\beta\rangle$ and fixes $|\alpha\rangle$. The diffusion operator is the reflection across the line through $|s\rangle$. The plane is invariant under both.

<Statement>
**Theorem.** The product of two reflections of a plane, across lines meeting at angle $\tfrac\theta2$, is a rotation by $\theta$. Hence $G = D\,O$ rotates the plane spanned by $|\alpha\rangle$ and $|\beta\rangle$ by $\theta$ towards $|\beta\rangle$, and

$$
G^k|s\rangle = \cos\!\big(\tfrac{2k+1}{2}\theta\big)\,|\alpha\rangle + \sin\!\big(\tfrac{2k+1}{2}\theta\big)\,|\beta\rangle .
$$
</Statement>

The probability of measuring a solution after $k$ iterations is therefore

$$
P_k = \sin^2\!\big(\tfrac{2k+1}{2}\theta\big).
$$

It is close to $1$ when $(2k+1)\tfrac\theta2 \approx \tfrac\pi2$. For $M \ll N$ we have $\tfrac\theta2 \approx \sqrt{M/N}$, so the right number of iterations is

$$
k \approx \frac{\pi}{4}\sqrt{\frac{N}{M}} .
$$

The rotation does not stop at the target. Iterating further turns the state away from $|\beta\rangle$ again and the success probability falls: with Grover's algorithm, doing more work can make the answer worse.

<Example>
Take $N = 4$ and $M = 1$. Then $\sin\tfrac\theta2 = \tfrac12$, so $\tfrac\theta2 = \tfrac\pi6$, and after one iteration the angle is $3 \cdot \tfrac\pi6 = \tfrac\pi2$: the state is exactly $|\beta\rangle$. One query finds one item among four with certainty, where a classical search needs $2.25$ queries on average.
</Example>
