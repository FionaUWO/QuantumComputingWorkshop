In lecture 7 each code was described twice: by its basis states, and by the observables that every code state satisfies with eigenvalue $+1$. The second description is far more compact, and it is the one that generalises.

### The Pauli group

The *Pauli group* $G_n$ on $n$ qubits consists of all tensor products of $I$, $X$, $Y$, $Z$ with an overall factor $\pm 1$ or $\pm i$. It has $4^{n+1}$ elements. We write $X_1 Z_3$ for $X \otimes I \otimes Z$ and so on. Three facts are used constantly:

- Every element squares to $\pm I$, and those that are Hermitian have eigenvalues $\pm 1$.
- Any two elements either commute or anticommute. Two Pauli products anticommute exactly when the number of positions where they hold different non-identity matrices is odd.
- The *weight* of an element is the number of positions where it is not the identity.

### Stabilizers

<Statement>
**Definition.** Let $S$ be a subgroup of $G_n$. The space *stabilized* by $S$ is

$$
V_S = \{\,|\psi\rangle : g|\psi\rangle = |\psi\rangle \text{ for all } g \in S\,\} .
$$
</Statement>

<Example>
The Bell state $\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}(|00\rangle + |11\rangle)$ satisfies $X_1 X_2|\psi\rangle = |\psi\rangle$ and $Z_1 Z_2|\psi\rangle = |\psi\rangle$, and it is the only state, up to phase, that does. So it is described by the two generators $X_1 X_2$ and $Z_1 Z_2$ instead of four amplitudes.
</Example>

For $V_S$ to be non-zero, two conditions are necessary. $S$ must not contain $-I$, since $-I|\psi\rangle = |\psi\rangle$ forces $|\psi\rangle = 0$. And $S$ must be abelian: if $g, h \in S$ anticommute then $gh|\psi\rangle = -hg|\psi\rangle$, again forcing $|\psi\rangle = 0$. These conditions are also sufficient, and the dimension can be read off.

<Statement>
**Proposition.** Let $S = \langle g_1, \dots, g_{n-k}\rangle$ be generated by $n - k$ independent commuting elements of $G_n$, with $-I \notin S$. Then $\dim V_S = 2^k$.
</Statement>

*Idea of proof.* Each $g_i$ has eigenvalues $\pm 1$ with eigenspaces of equal dimension, so the condition $g_1|\psi\rangle = |\psi\rangle$ halves the dimension. Because the generators commute and are independent, each further condition halves it again. $\qquad\blacksquare$

A subgroup with $n - k$ generators is specified by a list of $n - k$ Pauli strings, about $2n(n-k)$ bits, whereas a general subspace of dimension $2^k$ in $\mathbb{C}^{2^n}$ needs exponentially many numbers.
