1. Write out $\mathrm{QFT}_8$ in terms of $\omega = e^{2\pi i/8}$ and check that two of its columns are orthogonal.
2. Show that $\mathrm{QFT}_N^2$ sends $|j\rangle$ to $|{-j} \bmod N\rangle$, and deduce that $\mathrm{QFT}_N^4 = I$. What are the possible eigenvalues?
3. Draw the quantum Fourier transform circuit for $n = 3$ and multiply out its action on $|j_1 j_2 j_3\rangle$ to confirm the product form.
4. Compute $\mathrm{QFT}_8$ of $\tfrac12\big(|1\rangle + |3\rangle + |5\rangle + |7\rangle\big)$ directly and compare with the proposition on periodic states.
5. In one-bit phase estimation, what are the outcome probabilities for $\varphi = 0$, $\tfrac14$, $\tfrac12$? Which values of $\varphi$ can one control qubit identify with certainty?
6. Verify that $|u_s\rangle$ is an eigenvector of $U$ for $N = 15$, $x = 7$, $s = 1$ by writing out all four terms.
7. Find the continued fraction expansion and the convergents of $\tfrac{853}{2048}$. If this were the output of order finding for some $N \le 31$, what would you guess for $r$?
8. Carry out the classical part of Shor's algorithm for $N = 21$ with $x = 2$: find the order by hand, then the factors.
9. For $N = 15$, list the orders of all $x$ coprime to $15$ and count how many choices of $x$ lead to a factor. Compare with the bound $1 - 2^{-m}$.

<p class="reading">Reading: more problems of this kind are in Nielsen and Chuang, Chapter 5.</p>
