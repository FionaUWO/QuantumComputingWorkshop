Let $G = (V, E)$ be a graph with vertices $1, \dots, n$. A *cut* is a partition of the vertices into two sets, recorded as a bit string $z \in \{0,1\}^n$ with $z_i$ naming the side of vertex $i$. An edge is *cut* if its endpoints lie on different sides. The *MaxCut* problem asks for the largest number of edges that can be cut:

$$
C(z) = \sum_{(i,j) \in E} [\,z_i \ne z_j\,], \qquad C_{\max} = \max_{z} C(z) .
$$

Put $s_i = (-1)^{z_i} \in \{+1, -1\}$. Then $s_i s_j = -1$ exactly when the edge is cut, so $[z_i \ne z_j] = \tfrac12(1 - s_i s_j)$. Replacing $s_i$ by the operator $Z_i$ gives the *cost Hamiltonian*

$$
H_C = \sum_{(i,j) \in E} \tfrac12\big(I - Z_i Z_j\big), \qquad H_C|z\rangle = C(z)\,|z\rangle .
$$

<Statement>
**Proposition.** $H_C$ is diagonal in the computational basis with eigenvalues $C(z)$. Its largest eigenvalue is $C_{\max}$, attained by the maximum cuts. Equivalently, the maximum cuts are the ground states of the Ising Hamiltonian $\sum_{(i,j) \in E} Z_i Z_j$, with ground energy $|E| - 2C_{\max}$.
</Statement>

Flipping every bit swaps the two sides and leaves the cut unchanged: $C(\bar z) = C(z)$. In operator terms, $H_C$ commutes with $X^{\otimes n}$. Optimal solutions therefore come in pairs.

<Example>
For the triangle, at most two of the three edges can be cut, and the six strings other than $000$ and $111$ all achieve it: $C_{\max} = 2$. For the $4$-cycle, the alternating strings $0101$ and $1010$ cut all four edges: $C_{\max} = 4$. In general a graph has a cut containing every edge exactly when it is bipartite.
</Example>

### Hardness

MaxCut is NP-hard: an efficient algorithm for it would give one for every problem in NP. Approximation is the realistic goal. A uniformly random cut already cuts each edge with probability $\tfrac12$, so it achieves $\tfrac12|E| \ge \tfrac12 C_{\max}$ on average. The best known efficient classical algorithm, due to Goemans and Williamson, guarantees a cut of at least $0.878\,C_{\max}$, using a relaxation to a semidefinite programme. Any quantum method has to be judged against that number.
