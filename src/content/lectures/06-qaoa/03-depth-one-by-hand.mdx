For $p = 1$ the score can be computed in closed form. By linearity it is a sum over edges,

$$
F_1(\gamma, \beta) = \sum_{(u,v) \in E} \langle\gamma, \beta|\,\tfrac12(I - Z_u Z_v)\,|\gamma, \beta\rangle ,
$$

so it is enough to understand one edge at a time.

### A single edge

Let the graph be one edge on two qubits. The computation is a pleasant exercise with Pauli matrices, and the result is

$$
F_1(\gamma, \beta) = \tfrac12 + \tfrac12\,\sin 4\beta\,\sin\gamma .
$$

At $\gamma = \tfrac\pi2$, $\beta = \tfrac\pi8$ this equals $1$: the state is $\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}\big(|01\rangle + |10\rangle\big)$ up to phase, an equal superposition of the two maximum cuts. Depth one solves a single edge exactly. Notice that the output is a Bell state: QAOA reaches good cuts through entangled states.

### Regular graphs without triangles

<Statement>
**Theorem.** Let $G$ be a $d$-regular graph with no triangles. For every edge,

$$
\langle\gamma, \beta|\,\tfrac12(I - Z_u Z_v)\,|\gamma, \beta\rangle = \tfrac12 + \tfrac12\,\sin 4\beta\,\sin\gamma\,\cos^{d-1}\gamma ,
$$

so $F_1 = |E|\,\big(\tfrac12 + \tfrac12 \sin 4\beta \sin\gamma \cos^{d-1}\gamma\big)$.
</Statement>

The factor $\cos^{d-1}\gamma$ comes from the $d - 1$ other edges at each endpoint: their phases partly wash out the signal. Maximising, $\beta = \tfrac\pi8$ and $\tan^2\gamma = \tfrac{1}{d-1}$.

| Graph | $d$ | Best $F_1/\lvert E\rvert$ |
| --- | --- | --- |
| single edge | $1$ | $1$ |
| cycle | $2$ | $\tfrac34$ |
| $3$-regular, no triangles | $3$ | $\tfrac12 + \tfrac{1}{3\sqrt3} \approx 0.6924$ |

For the even cycle $C_{\max} = |E|$, so depth one reaches three quarters of the optimum. For cycles it is known that depth $p$ reaches $\tfrac{2p+1}{2p+2}$.

For $3$-regular graphs in general, depth one guarantees a cut of at least $0.6924\,C_{\max}$. That is a genuine worst-case guarantee, and it is well below the $0.878$ of the classical Goemans-Williamson algorithm. Depth one does not beat classical methods; the question is what happens as $p$ grows.

<p class="reading">Reading: Farhi, Goldstone and Gutmann (2014), Sections 3 to 5. The general depth-one formula is in Z. Wang, S. Hadfield, Z. Jiang and E. Rieffel, "Quantum approximate optimization algorithm for MaxCut: a fermionic view", Physical Review A 97 (2018).</p>
